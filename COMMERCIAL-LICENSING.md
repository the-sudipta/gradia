# Commercial Licensing

Gradia is publicly visible under a source-available non-commercial license. The
public [LICENSE](LICENSE) does not grant commercial-use rights.

## Activities requiring a commercial agreement

Examples include:

- institutional or company deployment connected to paid operations;
- sale, resale, rental, sublicensing, or paid distribution;
- a hosted or software-as-a-service offering;
- bundling Gradia with a commercial product or service;
- paid consultancy, implementation, support, or training based on Gradia; and
- internal use by a for-profit organization.

When uncertain, ask the Licensor before use.

## Inquiry process

1. Contact Sudipta Kumar through a private route on
   <https://github.com/the-sudipta>.
2. Describe the legal entity, intended use, territories, user count, deployment
   model, support needs, data responsibilities, and expected start date.
3. The parties discuss scope, monthly royalty or other fee, reporting, security,
   support, audit, term, payment timing, missed-payment consequences, termination,
   governing law, and dispute terms.
4. Qualified legal representatives review the draft.
5. Unless the Licensor agrees otherwise in writing, the final agreement is signed
   as two identical physical originals, one retained by each party.
6. Commercial rights begin only when the signed agreement says they begin and its
   commencement conditions are satisfied.

No repository issue, message, meeting, quotation, invoice, draft, download, or
verbal statement is itself a commercial license.

## Royalty and missed payments

There is no universal public royalty amount. The monthly royalty or alternative fee
must be negotiated for the actual deployment and written into the signed agreement.
The agreement must also define invoicing, taxes, currency, due dates, reporting,
audit rights, cure periods if any, suspension, termination, and post-termination
obligations.

If a required payment is missed, commercial use must stop whenever the signed
agreement requires suspension or termination. The public non-commercial license
does not authorize continued commercial use.

## Intellectual-property scope

Commercial licensing covers the rights the Licensor can lawfully grant in Gradia’s
protected code, documentation, artwork, branding, and other materials. Abstract
ideas and independently developed similar features are not automatically controlled
by copyright. Patent, trade-secret, trademark, contract, and database rights require
their own factual and legal analysis.

## Legal review

This document explains the intended business process; it is not a contract or legal
advice. The owner should engage qualified counsel before commercial negotiation,
signature, or enforcement.
